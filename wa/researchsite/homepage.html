Research Exemplar

When No One Is Looking

A guided experience through interview excerpts, saved content, and designed objects generated from digital behavior on social media.

Intro

A study of what we hide and what we reveal.

This project looks at the gap between what we show and what we keep to ourselves online. Through interviews, saved content, and a series of form studies, each person’s relationship to visibility is translated into a layered lamp.

This website moves through each participant’s words, artifacts, and patterns before arriving at the physical object generated from their data.

Participant 01

Jane Doe

“Interacting with those sorts of posts or saving stuff I resonate with is a way for me to recognize the reality of who I am and realize my insecurities”

Voice

“If it was only my friends, I probably wouldn’t care. But family is different.”

“Some of the things I save feel more honest than what I would ever post.”

“There’s a difference between what I publicly perform and what I privately hold onto.”

Artifacts

Selected examples of content the participant saved or resonated with.

Reading

Jane Doe's interview suggested a distinction between what is socially acceptable to show and what feels privately meaningful. Saved content functioned as a quieter archive of identity: less performative, more intimate.

This tension informed the lamp’s structure. The relationship between the inner and outer shell reflects the degree of separation between public presentation and private authenticity.

Form

Generated Lamp

The degree of contact between the inner and outer surfaces at the top edge represents reveal ratio. Greater connection suggests stronger alignment between private identity and outward expression.

Method

Interview excerpts, social media content, and quantitative patterns were used as the basis for form generation.

Rather than directly visualizing statistics, the project translates behavioral tension into a spatial relationship between an inner and outer shell.
