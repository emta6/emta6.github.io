About Participants Method

The Study

On Instagram, likes are public and saves are private. I interviewed five participants to understand how that affects what they choose to engage with.

Then I asked them to show me what they keep when no one is looking. Most of them hesitated.

Each interview became a lamp. The height, the pattern, the layers — all of it generated from their data. Six people. Six objects. Each one a portrait of a digital self.

Participant 01

She/her. 21. 5 hours of screen time a day.

"If I really like something, I won't like it. I'll automatically save it."

She thinks of Instagram more as a scrapbook than a platform — more observer than producer. But her saves tell a different story.

"I guess there's things you are taught to be ashamed of."
"There's a certain section about a certain someone for saved posts that remind me of them. I don't want to show you."

The Object

Height — 200mm. Layers — 4. Reveal ratio — 6%.

The Question

Can translating private digital behavior into physical form reveal anything true about who we are?

Five people showed me their private archives. They hesitated. Make of that what you will.
